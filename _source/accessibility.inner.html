Our commitment

VitalKick should work for everyone who wants to understand their own numbers, including people with low vision, colour blindness, limited hand movement, hearing loss or cognitive disabilities. We aim to meet WCAG 2.2 at Level AA, the web accessibility standard from the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), on this website and in the VitalKick app, and we design for people's real settings, such as the largest text size.

What we have built in

On this website

  • High-contrast light text on a dark background
  • Every coloured range bar has a written label for each band, such as "Typical" or "Above normal"
  • Keyboard access throughout, with a visible focus outline; the biomarker panels open and close with Enter and Escape and move with the arrow keys
  • Buttons and links sized for fingers, at least 44 by 44 points for main controls
  • Descriptive alternative text on images, and labels on every form field
  • If your device asks for reduced motion, spinning icons and transitions are turned off and scrolling is not smoothed
  • The layout reflows to a single column on phones and narrow windows
  • The FAQ opens one answer at a time and announces whether each question is open or closed to screen readers

In the app

  • Every reading has a spoken label for VoiceOver and TalkBack, such as "Systolic blood pressure, 134 millimeters of mercury"
  • Designed for the largest Dynamic Type and font size settings
  • Readings always show the number, the unit and a text label, never colour alone
  • Estimates are marked with a written badge, not only an amber colour
  • Plain-language explanations, written for a wide reading level
  • Every word goes through the app's translation system, with Spanish next

The scan itself

The scan needs your face to be visible to the front camera for 60 seconds. If holding the phone is hard, rest it on a stand, a table or a pillow at eye level. A family member or carer can hold the phone for you. Sitting still and staying quiet during the scan gives the best reading.

Known limitations

We would rather tell you than have you find out:

  • The 3D skin layers and the animated face scan on the home page are visual demonstrations. The same information is written out in text next to them, and the page shows a flat drawing on devices without 3D graphics.
  • The 60-second reel on the home page has music and no narration. What it shows is described in the text of the page.
  • Some scroll-driven sections may be harder to follow with screen magnifiers.
  • Some placeholder pages are still being written.

How we check

This statement reflects our own review of the website in October 2026, using keyboard-only navigation, reduced motion, phone and desktop layouts, and automated checks of the copy and code. It has not yet been tested end to end with screen readers or audited by an outside firm, and we will update this page when it is. We review it again with each major release.

Tell us about a barrier

If something on this website or in the app is hard to use, please call or text (305) 753-4452 or book a call. Tell us the page or screen, what you were trying to do, and the device or assistive technology you use. We will help you get what you need in another way, and we will work on a fix.
