How it works

A pulse, read from light.

  1. 01
    Blood changes the colour of skin

    Each heartbeat pushes a little more blood into the small vessels of the face, and blood absorbs green light.

  2. 02
    The camera records the change

    The forehead and cheeks get very slightly darker and lighter with every beat, far too little for the eye to see.

  3. 03
    The phone turns it into readings

    On the device, the signal becomes a pulse wave and then twelve readings. The video is discarded when the scan ends.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

The ones we hear most. Tap a question to open it.

Each heartbeat pushes a little more blood into the skin of your face. That changes the colour of your skin by an amount too small for your eyes to see, but a phone camera can pick it up.

During the 60-second scan, the rPPG engine follows those changes across your forehead and cheeks and turns them into a pulse wave. Your readings are worked out from that wave. rPPG stands for remote photoplethysmography: measuring blood flow with light, without touching you.

  • Sit down and rest for 5 minutes first.
  • Face a window or a lamp so the light is even on your face. Avoid strong light behind you.
  • Hold the phone at eye level, or rest it on something steady.
  • Keep still and do not talk during the scan.
  • Move hair, hats or glasses away from your forehead and cheeks.
  • Scan at about the same time each day so your trend compares like with like.
